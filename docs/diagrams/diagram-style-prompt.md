# Atlas diagram style prompt (reusable)

Workflow: **you generate a TEXT-FREE illustration from the prompt → upload it →
I add the labels, arrows and callouts in the Atlas style and place it in the
summary sheet / video.** Keep using the same STYLE BLOCK (and ideally the same
image tool/model) for every topic so all diagrams match.

---

## STYLE BLOCK — paste into every prompt, unchanged

> Clean, flat, modern educational biology illustration in a minimalist vector
> style. Smooth rounded shapes, gentle soft shadows and crisp light edges, no
> harsh outlines. A calm, cohesive, premium textbook-poster look. Muted palette:
> soft off-white background (#F7F7FB); indigo/violet (#5B5BD6) only for subtle
> structural accents; soft natural anatomical tones — rose/pink tissue, warm red
> for oxygen-rich blood, muted blue for oxygen-poor blood. Even, diffuse
> lighting. The subject is centred and isolated with generous empty margin around
> it. **IMPORTANT: the image must contain absolutely NO text, NO labels, NO
> words, letters, numbers, captions, arrows or annotations of any kind — only the
> illustration itself, with clear empty space left around and beside it so labels
> can be added afterwards.** High resolution, clean edges, transparent or plain
> off-white background.

Then add one **SUBJECT LINE** (below) + an aspect ratio (square 1:1 for spot
diagrams, 4:3 for scenes).

---

## SUBJECT LINES — Respiration in Humans (use these first)

**1. Respiratory system overview, with the larynx and a zoom bubble to the alveoli** (aspect 3:2, landscape)
> Subject: a clean front-on view of the human respiratory system. At the top sits
> the voice box (larynx) as a small structure directly above the windpipe. The
> windpipe (trachea) runs down the centre and divides into two main bronchi, one
> entering each lung, then branches repeatedly into finer and finer bronchioles
> like an upside-down tree, filling BOTH softly-shaded lungs evenly and
> symmetrically — the lungs contain NO stray clusters on their own. To one side
> of the lungs, there is a large circular magnified inset drawn like a round
> magnifying-glass zoom bubble, connected to the tip of one bronchiole by two thin
> tapering guide lines (a classic textbook "zoom-in" callout). ONLY inside that
> zoom bubble do we see the close-up: a realistic grape-like bunch of tiny rounded
> alveoli (air sacs) budding from the end of the bronchiole, a few of them wrapped
> in fine capillaries. Keep the composition balanced, with the lungs on one side
> and the zoom bubble on the other.
>
> (The larynx, the magnifying-glass circle and its two connector guide lines are
> part of the illustration and are fine — they are NOT text/arrows. Still
> absolutely no words, letters or numbers anywhere.)

## SUBJECT LINES — Cell Structure and Organisation (Topic 1)

**1. Animal cell and plant cell side by side, cut away** (aspect 3:2, landscape)
> Subject: two large cut-away cells drawn side by side with a clear gap between
> them. On the left, a typical ANIMAL cell — a soft rounded, slightly irregular
> shape. On the right, a typical PLANT cell — a tall rectangular box shape. Both
> are cut open to reveal, as smooth rounded organelles floating in the cytoplasm:
> a large central nucleus (with a small nucleolus inside), several bean-shaped
> mitochondria, a folded ribbon-like endoplasmic reticulum, a stack of flattened
> curved sacs for the Golgi body, and tiny dot-like ribosomes. The animal cell has
> a thin smooth outer cell membrane and only small scattered vacuoles. The plant
> cell additionally has a thick straight outer cell wall just outside its cell
> membrane, one big central sap vacuole taking up most of the middle (pushing the
> other organelles to the edges), and several green oval chloroplasts. Keep both
> cells clearly separated with generous empty margin all around and between them so
> labels can be added afterwards. Use the muted palette: rose/pink cytoplasm,
> indigo/violet nucleus, green chloroplasts, soft natural organelle tones.

**2. Alveolus gas exchange close-up** (aspect 1:1)
> Subject: an extreme close-up cross-section of one rounded alveolus (air sac)
> wrapped by a network of capillaries carrying red blood cells, showing the very
> thin alveolar wall pressed against the thin capillary wall beside it; a few
> blood cells visible inside the capillary.
