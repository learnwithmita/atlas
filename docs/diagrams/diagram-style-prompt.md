# Atlas diagram style prompt (reusable)

Workflow: **you generate a TEXT-FREE illustration from the prompt → upload it →
I add the labels, arrows and callouts in the Atlas style and place it in the
summary sheet / video.** Keep using the same STYLE BLOCK (and ideally the same
image tool/model) for every topic so all diagrams match.

---

## STYLE BLOCK — paste into every prompt, unchanged

> Clean, flat, modern educational biology illustration in a minimalist vector
> style. Smooth rounded shapes, gentle soft shadows and crisp light edges, no
> harsh outlines. A calm, cohesive, premium textbook-poster look. Muted palette:
> soft off-white background (#F7F7FB); indigo/violet (#5B5BD6) only for subtle
> structural accents; soft natural anatomical tones — rose/pink tissue, warm red
> for oxygen-rich blood, muted blue for oxygen-poor blood. Even, diffuse
> lighting. The subject is centred and isolated with generous empty margin around
> it. **IMPORTANT: the image must contain absolutely NO text, NO labels, NO
> words, letters, numbers, captions, arrows or annotations of any kind — only the
> illustration itself, with clear empty space left around and beside it so labels
> can be added afterwards.** High resolution, clean edges, transparent or plain
> off-white background.

Then add one **SUBJECT LINE** (below) + an aspect ratio (square 1:1 for spot
diagrams, 4:3 for scenes).

---

## SUBJECT LINES — Respiration in Humans (use these first)

**1. Respiratory system overview, with a zoom bubble to the alveoli** (aspect 3:2, landscape)
> Subject: a clean front-on view of the human respiratory system. The windpipe
> (trachea) runs down the centre and divides into two main bronchi, one entering
> each lung, then branches repeatedly into finer and finer bronchioles like an
> upside-down tree, filling BOTH softly-shaded lungs evenly and symmetrically —
> the lungs contain NO stray clusters on their own. To one side of the lungs,
> there is a large circular magnified inset drawn like a round magnifying-glass
> zoom bubble, connected to the tip of one bronchiole by two thin tapering guide
> lines (a classic textbook "zoom-in" callout). ONLY inside that zoom bubble do
> we see the close-up: a realistic grape-like bunch of tiny rounded alveoli (air
> sacs) budding from the end of the bronchiole, a few of them wrapped in fine
> capillaries. Keep the composition balanced, with the lungs on one side and the
> zoom bubble on the other.
>
> (The magnifying-glass circle and its two connector guide lines are part of the
> illustration and are fine — they are NOT text/arrows. Still absolutely no
> words, letters or numbers anywhere.)

**2. Alveolus gas exchange close-up** (aspect 1:1)
> Subject: an extreme close-up cross-section of one rounded alveolus (air sac)
> wrapped by a network of capillaries carrying red blood cells, showing the very
> thin alveolar wall pressed against the thin capillary wall beside it; a few
> blood cells visible inside the capillary.

## Reuse for later topics (just swap the subject line), e.g.
- Cell structure: "an animal cell and a plant cell side by side, cut away to show
  the nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, (plant: chloroplasts,
  vacuole, cell wall) as smooth rounded organelles."
- Heart/circulation: "a cross-section of the human heart showing four chambers
  and the main vessels, chambers in warm red / muted blue for oxygenated vs
  deoxygenated blood."
- Digestive system: "the human digestive tract from mouth to anus as one
  continuous labelled-free illustration inside a simplified torso."

Always keep the STYLE BLOCK identical and the **no-text rule** in every prompt.
