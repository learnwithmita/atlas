## Subtopic: Plant and Animal Cells (1a–1c)

| Front | Back |
|-------|------|
| What is the function of the cell membrane? | Partially permeable; controls what enters and leaves the cell. |
| How permeable is the cell membrane? | Partially permeable (not fully permeable). |
| What is the function of the cytoplasm? | A jelly-like medium where many chemical (metabolic) reactions take place. |
| What is the function of the nucleus? | Contains the genetic material (DNA); controls the cell's activities and cell division. |
| What is the cell wall made of, and what does it do? | Cellulose; gives the plant cell a fixed shape and support and stops it bursting. It is freely permeable. |
| What is the function of chloroplasts? | Contain chlorophyll, which absorbs light energy for photosynthesis. |
| What is the function of the large sap vacuole in a plant cell? | Stores cell sap and keeps the cell turgid (firm) for support. |
| What is the function of mitochondria? | Site of aerobic respiration; release energy for the cell. |
| What is the function of ribosomes? | Site of protein synthesis. |
| What is the function of the endoplasmic reticulum? | Transports substances (such as proteins) around the cell. |
| What is the function of the Golgi body? | Modifies, packages and transports proteins. |
| Name three structures found in plant cells but not animal cells. | Cell wall, chloroplasts, and a large permanent (sap) vacuole. |
| Name the structures found in BOTH animal and plant cells. | Cell membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi body. |
| How does the shape of animal and plant cells differ? | Animal cells are irregular/rounded; plant cells have a regular, fixed shape because of the cell wall. |
| What is the difference between the cell wall and the cell membrane? | Wall: cellulose, freely permeable, gives support (plant only). Membrane: partially permeable, controls entry/exit (both cells). |
| What is cell sap? | A solution of sugars and salts in water, stored in the plant vacuole. |
| Define "partially permeable". | Allows some substances to pass through but not others. |
| Which organelle is the "powerhouse" / site of aerobic respiration? | Mitochondria. |

## Subtopic: Cell Specialisation (1d)

| Front | Back |
|-------|------|
| What does it mean that a cell is "specialised"? | Its structure is adapted to carry out a particular function efficiently. |
| Why does a red blood cell have no nucleus? | So there is more room for haemoglobin, allowing it to carry more oxygen. |
| Why is a red blood cell a biconcave disc? | It gives a large surface area to volume ratio, so oxygen diffuses in and out quickly. |
| What does haemoglobin do? | Binds to and transports oxygen. |
| How is a root hair cell adapted for absorption? | A long, narrow projection gives a large surface area to absorb water (osmosis) and mineral ions (active transport). |
| Why does a root hair cell have many mitochondria? | To release energy for the active transport of mineral ions. |
| Why does a muscle cell have many mitochondria? | To release the large amount of energy needed for contraction. |
| State the rule for answering "how is this cell adapted?" questions. | Give the feature AND the reason it helps (feature + function). |
