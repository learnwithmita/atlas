# Movement of Substances — Flashcards

Derived from the in-depth notes. Subtopic: **Diffusion, Osmosis and Active Transport**.

| Front | Back |
|-------|------|
| Define diffusion. | The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration (down a concentration gradient). |
| Does diffusion require energy from the cell? | No — it is passive. |
| Name two factors that increase the rate of diffusion. | A steeper concentration gradient, a larger surface area, a shorter distance, or a higher temperature (any two). |
| Give one example of diffusion in the human body. | Oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged at the alveoli (or digested food is absorbed in the small intestine). |
| Define osmosis. | The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute) to lower water potential (concentrated), through a partially permeable membrane. |
| In osmosis, does water move to the dilute or the concentrated side? | To the concentrated side (from higher to lower water potential). |
| What is water potential, in simple terms? | How "free" the water is: a dilute solution has high water potential, a concentrated solution has low water potential. |
| In osmosis, what actually moves — water or the solute? | Only water molecules. |
| What kind of membrane is needed for osmosis? | A partially permeable membrane. |
| What happens to a plant cell in a dilute solution? | Water enters by osmosis, the vacuole swells, and the cell becomes turgid (firm); the wall stops it bursting. |
| What happens to a plant cell in a concentrated solution? | Water leaves; the cell becomes flaccid, then plasmolysed (the membrane pulls away from the wall). |
| What happens to a red blood cell in pure water? | It swells and bursts, because it has no cell wall. |
| What happens to a red blood cell in a concentrated solution? | It shrinks and becomes crinkled. |
| Why does a plant cell not burst in water, but a red blood cell does? | The plant cell has a strong cellulose cell wall that prevents bursting; the red blood cell has none. |
| Define active transport. | The movement of particles against the concentration gradient (from lower to higher concentration), using energy from respiration, through carrier proteins. |
| Does active transport require energy? | Yes — energy released by respiration. |
| Give two examples of active transport. | Uptake of mineral ions by root hair cells; uptake of glucose in the villi of the small intestine. |
| Why do root hair cells have many mitochondria? | To release energy for the active transport of mineral ions. |
| What two things make active transport different from diffusion? | It moves particles against the gradient, and it uses energy. |
| What do diffusion and osmosis have in common? | Both are passive (use no energy) and move substances down a gradient. |
