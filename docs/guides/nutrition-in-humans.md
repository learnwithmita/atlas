# Nutrition in Humans

**Topic at a glance.** This topic follows food through the **alimentary canal** (gut): how each organ handles it, how **enzymes** break large molecules into small soluble ones, how the small intestine is built to **absorb** them, and what the **liver** does with the nutrients afterwards. You also need the role of the **hepatic portal vein** and the effects of **alcohol**. Questions often ask you to state the function of a named organ, match an enzyme to its substrate and end-product, explain how a **villus** is adapted for absorption, or describe a role of the liver.

![Labelled human digestive system](../diagrams/nutrition/gut-anatomy.png)

Five things happen to food as it passes through the gut:

- **Ingestion** is taking food into the body (through the mouth).
- **Digestion** is breaking large, insoluble molecules into small, soluble ones.
- **Absorption** is the passage of these small molecules into the blood (and lacteal).
- **Assimilation** is using the absorbed nutrients inside cells (for energy, growth or storage).
- **Egestion** is removing undigested material as faeces through the anus.

---

## 4a — Functions of the parts of the digestive system

| Part | Main function |
|---|---|
| **Mouth** | **Ingestion**. Teeth chew food (physical digestion); **salivary amylase** begins **starch** digestion; the tongue rolls food into a ball to swallow. |
| **Salivary glands** | Release **saliva**, which contains **salivary amylase** (digests starch to maltose) and mucus to lubricate the food. |
| **Oesophagus** | Carries food from the mouth to the stomach by **peristalsis**. No digestion here. |
| **Stomach** | Muscular walls churn food; **gastric juice** supplies **hydrochloric acid** (kills bacteria, gives the right pH) and **protease (pepsin)** to digest **protein**. |
| **Duodenum** | First part of the small intestine. Receives **bile** (from the gall bladder) and **pancreatic enzymes**, which continue digestion. |
| **Pancreas** | Makes **amylase, protease and lipase** and releases them into the duodenum. |
| **Gall bladder** | **Stores bile** (made by the liver) and releases it into the duodenum. |
| **Liver** | **Makes bile**, which **emulsifies fats** (see 4f for its other roles). |
| **Ileum** | Second part of the small intestine. **Absorbs** the digested food through its **villi**. |
| **Colon (large intestine)** | **Absorbs water** from the undigested material, leaving semi-solid faeces. |
| **Rectum** | **Stores faeces** before they are removed. |
| **Anus** | **Egestion**: faeces leave the body here. |

> Memory hook: enzymes are made in the **mouth, stomach and pancreas**; **bile** is made in the **liver** and stored in the **gall bladder**; absorption happens in the **ileum**, water is reclaimed in the **colon**.

---

## 4b — Peristalsis

**Peristalsis** is the **rhythmic, wave-like contraction of the muscles** in the wall of the alimentary canal that **mixes** the food with digestive juices and **propels** it along the gut.

- The **circular muscles** behind the food contract while those in front relax, squeezing the food forward.
- It works even when you are lying down or upside down, because it does not rely on gravity.

---

## 4c — Digestive enzymes

Enzymes break large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble ones that can be absorbed. Learn each enzyme with its **substrate** and its **end-product**.

| Enzyme | Where it is made | Substrate | End-product |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Amylase** | salivary glands, pancreas | **starch** | **maltose** |
| **Maltase** | small intestine | **maltose** | **glucose** |
| **Protease** (e.g. pepsin, trypsin) | stomach, pancreas | **protein** | **amino acids** |
| **Lipase** | pancreas | **fats (lipids)** | **fatty acids + glycerol** |

- **Bile** is **not** an enzyme. It **emulsifies** fats, breaking large fat droplets into many small ones. This gives a **larger surface area** for **lipase** to work on, speeding up fat digestion. Bile is also **alkaline**, so it neutralises the acid from the stomach and gives enzymes in the duodenum the right pH.
- The end-products (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol) are small and soluble, so they can be **absorbed**.

---

## 4d — How a villus is adapted for absorption

The ileum wall is folded into millions of tiny finger-like **villi** (each villus is covered in even smaller **microvilli**). This is where digested food is absorbed.

![Labelled villus showing microvilli, epithelium, capillary and lacteal](../diagrams/nutrition/villus.png)

A villus is well suited to absorption because it has:

- **A very large surface area.** Millions of villi, each with microvilli, give a huge surface area so more food is absorbed faster.
- **A thin wall, one cell thick (the epithelium).** This is a **short diffusion distance** into the blood.
- **A good blood supply (dense capillary network).** Blood carries absorbed **glucose and amino acids** away quickly, keeping a **steep concentration gradient** so diffusion continues.
- **A lacteal** in the centre, which absorbs the products of fat digestion (**fatty acids and glycerol**).

> Blood capillary takes **glucose and amino acids**; the **lacteal** takes **fatty acids and glycerol**. Keep these two routes separate in your answers.

---

## 4e — The hepatic portal vein

The **hepatic portal vein** carries blood **rich in absorbed nutrients** from the **small intestine** to the **liver**. This means the liver receives, and can act on, the nutrients first before the blood travels to the rest of the body.

---

## 4f — The role of the liver

The liver is the body's processing centre for absorbed nutrients. You need these roles:

- **Regulating glucose.** It converts **excess glucose to glycogen** for storage, and converts **glycogen back to glucose** when blood glucose falls.
- **Fat digestion.** It **makes bile**, which emulsifies fats (bile is stored in the gall bladder).
- **Metabolism of amino acids and formation of urea.** Excess amino acids cannot be stored, so the liver removes their nitrogen-containing part (**deamination**) to form **urea**, which the kidneys excrete.
- **Breakdown of alcohol.** The liver breaks down alcohol, which is toxic.
- **Breakdown of hormones.** The liver breaks down hormones once they have done their job.

---

## 4g — Effects of alcohol

Alcohol is a **depressant**: it slows down the activity of the brain.

- **On the brain (short term):** **increased reaction time** (slower responses) and **reduced self-control**.
- **Long-term effects of heavy drinking:** **liver disease** (cirrhosis, as liver cells are damaged) and **brain damage**.
- **Social implications:** accidents (for example drink-driving), **addiction (dependence)**, and problems with family, work or money.

## Exam technique
- For "state the function" of an organ, give its **one key job** in the right word: ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation or egestion.
- For enzymes, always pair the **substrate with its end-product** (for example protease: protein to amino acids).
- When explaining the villus, link each feature to **why** it helps: large surface area, short diffusion distance, steep gradient.
- Describe bile as **emulsifying** fats to give a **larger surface area for lipase**, and say it is **not** an enzyme.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Calling **bile** an enzyme. It is not; it **emulsifies** fat and **neutralises** acid.
- Saying the villus "breaks down" food. The villus **absorbs** already-digested food; it does not digest it.
- Mixing up the two absorption routes: **glucose and amino acids** go into the **blood capillary**; **fatty acids and glycerol** go into the **lacteal**.
- Saying the **stomach** absorbs food. Most absorption is in the **ileum**; the **colon** absorbs water.
- Writing that the liver "stores glucose". It stores it as **glycogen**.

## Sample questions and model answers

**How to approach these questions.** Use the exact terms the mark scheme wants: name the enzyme, its substrate and end-product; use **absorption**, **large surface area**, **short diffusion distance** and **concentration gradient** for the villus; and say **emulsifies** for bile. A **(1)** shows where each mark is awarded.

**Question 1.** Name the enzyme that digests starch, and state its end-product. **[2]**

*Answer:* Amylase (1); it produces maltose (1).

**Question 2.** Explain two ways a villus is adapted for the efficient absorption of digested food. **[4]**

*Answer:* It has a large surface area from the many villi and microvilli (1), so more food is absorbed (1); its wall is only one cell thick (1), giving a short diffusion distance into the blood (1).

**Question 3.** Explain how bile helps in the digestion of fats, even though it is not an enzyme. **[3]**

*Answer:* Bile emulsifies fats (1), breaking large fat droplets into many small droplets (1); this gives a larger surface area for lipase to act on, speeding up fat digestion (1).

**Question 4.** State the role of the liver in controlling blood glucose. **[2]**

*Answer:* It converts excess glucose to glycogen for storage (1), and converts glycogen back to glucose when blood glucose is low (1).
