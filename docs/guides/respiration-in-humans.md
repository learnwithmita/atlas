# Respiration in Humans

**Topic at a glance.** This is one of the most reliably tested topics in the Biology paper. It covers how air is moved in and out of the lungs, how oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged at the alveoli, how the body releases energy from glucose (with and without oxygen), and how tobacco smoke damages the system. "Explain" questions on the **alveolus**, the **breathing mechanism** and the **oxygen debt** come up most often.

## 6a — identify the larynx, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli and associated capillaries and state their functions in human gaseous exchange

**What you must know.** Air passes: **larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**. (Gaseous exchange means oxygen moving into the blood and carbon dioxide moving out.)
- **Larynx** (voice box): air passes through it on the way to the trachea; it also contains the vocal cords.
- **Trachea** (windpipe): carries air to the bronchi; held open by **rings of cartilage**; lined with **ciliated epithelium** (a surface layer of cells carrying tiny hairs called cilia). Mucus **traps** dust and microbes; the cilia **sweep** them up to the throat.
- **Bronchi**: carry air from the trachea into each lung.
- **Bronchioles**: smaller tubes that carry air to the alveoli.
- **Alveoli**: the air sacs where **gaseous exchange** happens.
- **Associated capillaries**: carry blood close to the alveoli so oxygen is picked up and carbon dioxide is released; they help maintain the diffusion gradient.

**Keywords.** **larynx**, **trachea**, **bronchi**, **bronchioles**, **alveoli**, **capillaries**, **cartilage**, **ciliated epithelium**, **gaseous exchange**.

**Examiner wants.** The correct order of structures and the function of each. **Common mistakes.** Confusing bronchi (into lungs) with bronchioles (to alveoli); forgetting the cartilage and cilia roles of the trachea.

## 6b — describe the process of breathing and the role of cilia, diaphragm, ribs and internal and external intercostal muscles

**What you must know.** Breathing (ventilation) moves air in and out by changing the volume and pressure of the thoracic cavity.

*Inspiration (breathing in):*
- The **external intercostal muscles contract** and the internal intercostals relax, moving the **ribs upwards and outwards**.
- The **diaphragm muscles contract**, so the diaphragm **flattens** and moves down.
- The **volume of the thoracic cavity increases**, so the **pressure decreases below atmospheric pressure**, and air is drawn into the lungs.

*Expiration (breathing out):*
- The **internal intercostal muscles contract** and the external intercostals relax, moving the **ribs downwards and inwards**.
- The **diaphragm muscles relax**, so the diaphragm **domes up**.
- The **volume of the thoracic cavity decreases**, so the **pressure increases above atmospheric pressure**, and air is forced out.

The internal and external intercostal muscles are **antagonistic** — they work in opposite directions.

**Role of cilia.** The trachea and bronchi are lined with **ciliated epithelium**. Mucus traps dust and microbes; the **cilia beat to sweep the mucus up** to the throat, where it is swallowed, keeping the airways clean.

**Keywords.** **external/internal intercostal muscles**, **diaphragm contracts/flattens**, **thoracic cavity**, **volume increases/decreases**, **pressure below/above atmospheric**, **antagonistic**, **cilia**, **mucus**.

**Examiner wants.** A clear cause→effect chain: muscle action → volume change → pressure change → air movement. **Common mistakes.** Saying the diaphragm "moves up" during inspiration; saying the lungs "suck" air in instead of explaining the pressure difference.

## 6c — explain how the structure of an alveolus is suited for its function of gaseous exchange

**What you must know.** The alveolus is adapted for fast, efficient diffusion of gases:
- **Large surface area** (millions of alveoli) → more gas diffuses at once.
- **Wall is one cell thick**, and the **capillary wall is also one cell thick** → a **short diffusion distance** for gases.
- **Moist lining** → gases dissolve before diffusing across.
- **Dense network of capillaries** (good blood supply) → maintains a **steep concentration (diffusion) gradient**.
- **Ventilation** (continual breathing) also keeps the gradient steep by refreshing the air.

**Keywords.** **large surface area**, **thin wall / one cell thick**, **short diffusion distance**, **moist**, **steep concentration gradient**, **dense capillary network**.

**Examiner wants.** Each feature **linked to why it helps** (feature → function), not just a list. **Common mistakes.** Listing features without explaining them; writing "thin" without "short diffusion distance".

## 6d — state the major toxic components of tobacco smoke – nicotine, tar and carbon monoxide, and describe their effects on health

**What you must know.**
- **Nicotine**: addictive; **raises heart rate and blood pressure**, which can increase the risk of heart disease.
- **Tar**: a **carcinogen** (cancer-causing substance) → causes **lung cancer**; paralyses and destroys cilia and stimulates extra mucus → mucus builds up → **chronic bronchitis**; damages alveoli walls → **emphysema** (reduced surface area for gaseous exchange).
- **Carbon monoxide**: combines **irreversibly** with **haemoglobin** in red blood cells to form **carboxyhaemoglobin**, and binds more strongly than oxygen does, reducing the blood's **oxygen-carrying capacity**; less oxygen reaches the tissues and the heart must work harder.

**Keywords.** **nicotine**, **tar**, **carbon monoxide**, **carcinogen**, **haemoglobin**, **red blood cells**, **carboxyhaemoglobin**, **irreversibly**, **chronic bronchitis**, **emphysema**.

**Examiner wants.** Each component matched to a **distinct** effect. **Common mistakes.** Mixing up tar (cancer, bronchitis, emphysema) with carbon monoxide (reduces oxygen transport).

## 6e — define aerobic respiration in human cells and state the equation, in words and symbols

**What you must know.** **Aerobic respiration** is the release of energy by the **breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen**.
- **Word equation:** glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (+ energy released)
- **Symbol equation:** C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O (+ energy released)

**Keywords.** **release of energy**, **breakdown of glucose**, **presence of oxygen**.

**Examiner wants.** The words "**released**" and "**presence of oxygen**" in the definition, and a correctly balanced symbol equation. **Common mistakes.** An unbalanced equation (forgetting the 6s); leaving "**in the presence of oxygen**" out of the definition; or writing lactic acid (the anaerobic product) instead of carbon dioxide and water.

## 6f — define anaerobic respiration in human cells and state the word equation

**What you must know.** **Anaerobic respiration** in human cells is the release of energy by the **breakdown of glucose in the absence of oxygen**. It releases **much less energy** than aerobic respiration.
- **Word equation:** glucose → lactic acid (+ energy released)

**Keywords.** **absence of oxygen**, **lactic acid**, **less energy**.

**Examiner wants.** "**absence of oxygen**" and the product "**lactic acid**" (human cells). **Common mistakes.** Giving the yeast equation (glucose → alcohol + carbon dioxide) for human cells.

## 6g — explain why cells respire anaerobically during vigorous exercise resulting in an oxygen debt that is removed by rapid, deep breathing after exercise

**What you must know** — follow the chain:
1. During vigorous exercise, muscles need energy faster than oxygen can be supplied, so there is **insufficient oxygen**.
2. Muscle cells respire **anaerobically as well as aerobically** to release extra energy quickly.
3. This produces **lactic acid**, which builds up and causes muscle fatigue.
4. The **oxygen debt** is the extra oxygen needed afterwards to **oxidise (break down) the accumulated lactic acid** into carbon dioxide and water.
5. The person keeps breathing **rapidly and deeply** after stopping (and the heart rate stays high) to take in this extra oxygen and repay the oxygen debt.

**Keywords.** **insufficient oxygen**, **anaerobic respiration**, **lactic acid**, **oxygen debt**, **oxidise/break down lactic acid**.

**Examiner wants.** The full link: insufficient oxygen → anaerobic respiration → lactic acid → oxygen debt → repaid by deep breathing. **Common mistakes.** Saying lactic acid is "breathed out"; forgetting that the extra oxygen oxidises the lactic acid.

## High-frequency focus
The three outcomes that carry the most marks in "explain" questions are:
- **6c** — alveolus adaptations (feature → function).
- **6b** — the breathing mechanism (volume/pressure chain).
- **6g** — anaerobic respiration and the oxygen debt.
Spend the most time writing full-sentence explanations for these three.

## Exam technique
- **State / identify** → a short, exact answer; no explanation needed (name the structure, give the equation).
- **Describe** → say what happens, in order (e.g. the sequence of events during inspiration).
- **Explain** → give reasons; every point must be *linked* to a consequence (feature → why it helps).

**Worked model answer (4 marks).** *"Describe two structural features of an alveolus and explain how each adapts it for efficient gaseous exchange."*
> The alveolus wall is **only one cell thick**, which gives a **short diffusion distance** so oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse across quickly (1 + 1). The alveoli are surrounded by a **dense network of capillaries** that carries oxygenated blood away and brings deoxygenated blood high in carbon dioxide, **maintaining a steep concentration gradient** for diffusion (1 + 1).

Notice each *feature* is paired with *why it helps* — that pairing is what earns full marks.
