# Nutrition in Humans — Flashcards

Curated term to answer cards derived from the in-depth notes.

## Human Digestive System
<!-- subtopic: c08a41a1-4b13-4121-a942-3647743657bc -->

| Front | Back |
|-------|------|
| What is ingestion? | Taking food into the body through the mouth. |
| What is digestion? | Breaking large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble ones. |
| What is absorption? | The passage of small, soluble food molecules into the blood (and lacteal). |
| What is assimilation? | Using absorbed nutrients inside cells, for example for energy, growth or storage. |
| What is egestion? | Removing undigested material as faeces through the anus. |
| What does the mouth do in digestion? | Teeth chew food (physical digestion) and salivary amylase begins starch digestion. |
| What enzyme is in saliva, and what does it do? | Salivary amylase, which digests starch to maltose. |
| What is the function of the oesophagus? | It carries food from the mouth to the stomach by peristalsis. |
| What does the stomach do? | Churns food; its acid and protease (pepsin) digest protein. |
| Why does the stomach contain hydrochloric acid? | It kills bacteria and gives the right pH for protease (pepsin) to work. |
| What is the function of the pancreas? | It makes amylase, protease and lipase, released into the duodenum. |
| What is the role of the gall bladder? | It stores bile (made by the liver) and releases it into the duodenum. |
| Where is most food absorbed? | In the ileum (small intestine), through the villi. |
| What does the colon (large intestine) do? | Absorbs water from the undigested material. |
| What is the function of the rectum? | It stores faeces before they are egested. |
| What is peristalsis? | Rhythmic, wave-like contractions of the gut muscles that mix and push food along. |
| Give the substrate and end-product of amylase. | Starch to maltose. |
| Give the substrate and end-product of maltase. | Maltose to glucose. |
| Give the substrate and end-product of protease. | Protein to amino acids. |
| Give the substrate and end-product of lipase. | Fats (lipids) to fatty acids and glycerol. |
| Is bile an enzyme? | No. Bile emulsifies fats and neutralises stomach acid. |
| What does it mean that bile emulsifies fat? | It breaks large fat droplets into many small ones, giving a larger surface area for lipase. |

## Absorption and Assimilation
<!-- subtopic: c298f263-b8ca-4704-b8ef-0fd1ff0ecc02 -->

| Front | Back |
|-------|------|
| What is a villus? | A tiny finger-like projection of the ileum wall where digested food is absorbed. |
| State three ways a villus is adapted for absorption. | Large surface area (many villi and microvilli); wall one cell thick; good blood supply; a lacteal. |
| Why is the villus wall being one cell thick useful? | It gives a short diffusion distance into the blood. |
| How do blood capillaries help absorption? | They carry glucose and amino acids away quickly, keeping a steep concentration gradient. |
| What does the blood capillary absorb? | Glucose and amino acids. |
| What does the lacteal absorb? | Fatty acids and glycerol (the products of fat digestion). |
| What do microvilli do? | They increase the surface area for faster absorption. |
| What does the hepatic portal vein carry, and to where? | Blood rich in absorbed nutrients, from the small intestine to the liver. |
| How does the liver control blood glucose? | It converts excess glucose to glycogen, and glycogen back to glucose when needed. |
| What is deamination? | The removal of the nitrogen-containing part of excess amino acids in the liver, forming urea. |
| What is formed when the liver breaks down excess amino acids? | Urea (excreted by the kidneys). |
| State two roles of the liver other than regulating glucose. | Makes bile; forms urea from amino acids; breaks down alcohol; breaks down hormones. |
| Where is bile made and where is it stored? | Made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder. |
| Why is alcohol described as a depressant? | It slows down the activity of the brain. |
| Give two short-term effects of alcohol on the brain. | Increased reaction time and reduced self-control. |
| Give two long-term effects of heavy drinking. | Liver disease (cirrhosis) and brain damage. |
| Give one social implication of alcohol misuse. | Accidents, addiction, or family, work or money problems. |
